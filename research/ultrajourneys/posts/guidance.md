Himalayan Traverse Planning

 July 16, 2023

 Peter Van Geit

Based on my extensive exploration of the Western Indian Himalayas I guide fellow International alpine style hikers in planning and executing their Himalayan traverses as per the seasons, identifying a realistic schedule, advising on suitable gears, planning night halts, food supply points and dynamically rerouting as per inclement weather. Some hikers follow Open Street Maps mapped routes, more adventurous ones can explore still unmapped trails via legacy Survey of India maps and digitally map these into OSM for the benefit of the outdoor community. We discuss elaborately in advance to plan the route, strategy and challenges and stay in touch during the actual traverse rerouting if necessary as per actual weather (snow) conditions. Support my efforts on mapping the Himalayas and guiding Himalayan explorers through Patreon.

Traverse / Season Planner

The Western Indian Himalayas consists of several beautiful regions, each to be visited during the right season. Each region can be explored via a unique traverse that hops across many valleys and mountain passes in that region (different colors above). We can also hike across multiple regions by combining individual traverses as per the right seasons. Blog post

Trans Himalaya

The Himalayas stretch out across a vast region spanning many countries from Arunachal in the Southeast to Kyrgyzstan in the far Northwest. In this post we present a multi-year trans Himalaya traverse over 9 thousand km across 6 countries. Note that this only covers a logical hiking route, we additionally need to plan for seasons (monsoon, winter snow) and physical access (VISAs, permits). Blog post

Laurent Berthod (Canada) – Afghanistan, Tajikistan, Kyrgistan, 2026

Shortly after completing my 2023-24 winter Himalayan Traverse (2600km, 126 000m+), I set my sights on its lesser-known neighbors : the Pamir and Tian Shan ranges. Sparsely populated, extremely vast and still relatively unknown, the region was a natural choice for my next project. When coming up with an idea for an adventure, I like to plan the journey by imagining a story in my mind, a bit like how one would go about making a film or a book. I try to divide it into chapters, each with their own atmospheres, encounters and challenges. To thread a coherent narrative, I am looking to craft a continuous line that flows through the various landscapes and connects remote settlements, creating opportunities to naturally meet locals along the way. Having a defined path gives me a clear sense of direction and serves as a foundation upon which I can let the unknown happen. Blog post

Camille Augey (France) – Shimla 2 Leh, 2026

My name is Camille Augey, 35, from France. I started planning this Himalayan traverse right after completing a 760-kilometre crossing of the French Alps in the summer of 2025. The French Alps have marked trails, huts, and plenty of fellow hikers. This time I wanted to take on a different kind of adventure. One of the important criteria was to undertake this journey solo and without a guide. This, as well as the seasonal conditions, guided my choice towards the Indian Himalayas, more specifically the states of Himachal Pradesh and Ladakh. Blog post

My name is Johan Solberg, and growing up in Norway, hiking was a natural part of my life.
