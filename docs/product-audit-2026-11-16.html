3ACE™ mathematical validation

How it works today

  • Person: each answer A–E scores 1–5. Two-question axes combine and lean to the weaker answer when the two are far apart. "Not sure" and unanswered both score a cautious 1. Calibration (how long since you trained, how often you train now) and optional health answers lower axes by fixed multipliers, never by more than half. Result: 1–5 on every axis, total 8–40.
  • Adventure: hand-set 0–5 per axis, with 0 meaning not needed. Ten adventures have no hand-set profile and use an estimate built from their hand-typed difficulty, type and altitude.
  • Match: a weighted average over the axes the adventure needs (weight = requirement). Full credit when met, 75% one level short, 40% two short, 10% three or more. Ready ≥ 80, Stretch ≥ 55.
  • Rank: Pathfinder 8–15, Navigator 16–23, Trailblazer 24–31, Vanguard 32–39, Apex 40 only.

Defect found and fixed: a strong profile could hide a dangerous gap

Because the score is an average, being strong everywhere else outweighed one large shortfall. Altitude 1 with 3s elsewhere was rated Ready (85%) for Hampta Pass at 4,298 m, and Stamina 1 was Ready for Sar Pass. Across every one-weak-axis profile against every adventure, 297 of 2,832 pairs were "Ready" despite a 3-level gap, and 610 despite a 2-level gap.

Fix: the largest single gap now caps the result. Two levels short on any axis is at best a Stretch; three or more is Not yet. The weighting is unchanged, and stored profiles need no migration. Every page, the map, groups and Compass inherit it because they all call the same function. After: 0 of 2,832. Tested at the boundaries for every adventure and axis, and for monotonicity (raising an axis never lowers a match).

Where the framework is weak (recommendations, not changed)

  • Difficulty bands are compressed at the top. Difficulty comes from the sum of the 8 axes (≤8 Easy, ≤14 Moderate, ≤20 Hard, ≤26 Advanced, above that Extreme). A Himalayan trek starts near 2 per axis, so 54 of 59 adventures land on Hard or above: 1 Easy, 4 Moderate, 24 Hard, 9 Advanced, 21 Extreme. Kedarkantha, a standard first Himalayan trek, is "Hard". Everest Base Camp (non-technical) sits in the same "Extreme" as Mera Peak (a 6,476 m climb). Recommendation: derive difficulty from the highest single demand plus the sum, or raise the bands. This changes labels only, not anyone's stored profile. It needs your approval because it changes what every page says.
  • Ten adventures run on estimated profiles built from their hand-typed difficulty. The Manali–Leh ride (5,359 m passes) estimates to Stamina 1, Strength 0, Agility 0, so it's rated "Moderate" and almost anyone is "Ready" for it. Others: Ladakh Circuit, Zanskar, Spiti bike, Spiti cycling, Lobuche, Mera Peak, Doodhpathri, Ranthan Kharak, Surya Top. FAIL These need hand-set profiles from someone who knows the routes.
  • The rank is a sum, not readiness. Apex needs a perfect 40, and a Vanguard can still be Not yet for an altitude trek. The rank is fine as a badge, but it shouldn't be shown as capability. The match verdicts now handle that correctly.
  • False precision. A match like "81%" comes from coarse 1–5 self-reports. Leading with the verdict and the named gaps, rather than the percentage, would be more honest.
  • Overlapping and ambiguous axes. "Water" means swimming for rafting but stream crossings for treks (Pin Parvati 4). "Focus" is described as comfort with heights and exposure, which overlaps "Nerve" (remoteness). Clearer one-line definitions on the ACE page would help.
  • Two difficulty fields. Each record had a hand-typed difficulty that disagreed with its ACE-derived one on 31 of 59 adventures, and Adventurepedia, site search and the medical cautions read the hand-typed one. Everest Base Camp was "Moderate" in its guide and "Extreme" on its page. FIXED Everything now reads one shared difficultyOf(). The hand-typed field only feeds the estimates above.

Root causes fixed

  • Keyword engine answered questions. Any message naming a place or activity skipped the AI, so permit, training, comparison, budget, altitude and group questions got lists. Those now go to the AI.
  • Unrelated cards added to answers. When the AI answered in words, the server attached random keyword matches. It now attaches only adventures the visitor named.
  • Dishonest fallback. When the free-tier model was rate-limited, the fallback said "Here are some adventures that match" whatever was asked. Now questions get an honest "can't answer right now" with a pointer to Adventurepedia, and searches say plainly when a limit (a weekend, a level) had to be relaxed. An "above 5,000 m" altitude limit is also enforced.
  • Prompt invited invention. The prompt told the model to name "planned locations" for activities that don't exist yet (diving in the Andamans, and so on). Now it must not promise them, and must not state prices, operators, dates, permits or conditions as fact.
  • Unchecked input. The browser could send "system" messages and unlimited history and length (prompt injection and cost risk). Now only the last 12 user/assistant turns are sent, each capped at 1,500 characters.

Latency: keyword answers under 120 ms; AI answers 1.2–3.1 s. The in-memory rate limiter (20 messages per 5 minutes per IP) is per server instance, a known open item.
